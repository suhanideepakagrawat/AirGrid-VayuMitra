## Which pollutants the index covers

The National Air Quality Index is computed over eight pollutants: PM10, PM2.5, NO2,
SO2, CO, O3, NH3 and Pb. An AQI value is published only when data are available for a
minimum of three pollutants, of which at least one must be PM10 or PM2.5.

## How the index is calculated

Each pollutant is first converted to a sub-index on a 0 to 500 scale using published
breakpoint tables, by linear interpolation between the breakpoints that bracket the
measured concentration. The AQI for a location is then the **maximum** of the available
sub-indices, not their average. The pollutant producing that maximum is reported as the
dominant or responsible pollutant.

## Averaging period

Sub-indices are computed from averaged concentrations, not from instantaneous readings.
PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, NH3 and Pb use a **24-hour average**. CO and O3 use the maximum
**8-hour rolling average** within the day. A minimum data completeness of 16 of 24 hours
is required for a 24-hour average to be considered valid.

## The six bands

| Band | AQI range | Health note |
|---|---|---|
| Good | 0-50 | Minimal impact. |
| Satisfactory | 51-100 | Minor breathing discomfort to sensitive people. |
| Moderate | 101-200 | Breathing discomfort to people with lung disease such as asthma, and discomfort to people with heart disease, children and older adults. |
| Poor | 201-300 | Breathing discomfort to most people on prolonged exposure. |
| Very Poor | 301-400 | Respiratory illness on prolonged exposure. |
| Severe | 401-500 | Respiratory effects even on healthy people; serious health impacts on people with lung or heart disease. Effects may be experienced even during light physical activity. |

## Sensitive groups

Children, older adults, pregnant women, and people with asthma, chronic obstructive
pulmonary disease or cardiovascular disease experience effects at lower concentrations
than the general population, and health advisories for these groups are issued at a
lower AQI than for the general public.
