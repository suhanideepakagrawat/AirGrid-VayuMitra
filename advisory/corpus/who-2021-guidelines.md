## Guideline values

The 2021 update tightened the 2005 guideline values substantially in response to
evidence of harm at lower concentrations than previously established.

| Pollutant | Averaging period | Guideline value |
|---|---|---|
| PM2.5 | Annual | 5 ug/m3 |
| PM2.5 | 24-hour | 15 ug/m3 |
| PM10 | Annual | 15 ug/m3 |
| PM10 | 24-hour | 45 ug/m3 |
| NO2 | Annual | 10 ug/m3 |
| NO2 | 24-hour | 25 ug/m3 |
| SO2 | 24-hour | 40 ug/m3 |
| O3 | Peak season, 8-hour | 60 ug/m3 |
| CO | 24-hour | 4 mg/m3 |

## Interim targets

Because many regions are far above the guideline values, WHO defines interim targets as
staged milestones. For 24-hour PM2.5 the interim targets are 75, 50, 37.5 and 25 ug/m3.
Progress toward an interim target is itself associated with measurable health benefit.

## Populations at higher risk

Children, older adults, pregnant women, and people with pre-existing respiratory or
cardiovascular disease experience effects at lower exposures. WHO notes that no threshold
has been identified below which fine particulate matter causes no harm, which is why
guidance for sensitive groups is issued more conservatively than for the general public.

## Health effects of short-term exposure

Short-term exposure to elevated particulate matter is associated with aggravation of
asthma, increased respiratory symptoms, reduced lung function, and increases in hospital
admissions and emergency department visits for respiratory and cardiovascular causes.
