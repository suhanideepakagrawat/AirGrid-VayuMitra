## What GRAP is

The Graded Response Action Plan is a statutory, pre-agreed ladder of emergency measures
for Delhi and the National Capital Region. Stages are invoked by the Commission for Air
Quality Management on the basis of AQI, and increasingly on the basis of **forecast** AQI
so that measures take effect before the threshold is crossed rather than after.

## Stage I - Poor (AQI 201 to 300)

Dust control at construction and demolition sites, mechanised road sweeping and water
sprinkling, strict enforcement against open waste burning, action against visibly
polluting vehicles, and enforcement of the ban on firecrackers. Citizens are advised to
keep vehicle engines tuned, maintain correct tyre pressure, and not idle at signals.

## Stage II - Very Poor (AQI 301 to 400)

All Stage I measures continue. Additionally: intensified mechanised sweeping on
identified roads, targeted action at identified air pollution hotspots, regulated use of
diesel generator sets, increased parking fees to discourage private transport, and
augmented public transport services. Citizens with heart or respiratory conditions are
advised to avoid prolonged outdoor exertion.

## Stage III - Severe (AQI 401 to 450)

All earlier measures continue. Additionally: a ban on non-essential construction and
demolition activity, closure of stone crushers and mining, restrictions on certain
categories of non-essential goods vehicles entering the region, and state discretion to
move schooling for younger classes to a hybrid mode. Citizens are advised to avoid
outdoor physical activity.

## Stage IV - Severe Plus (AQI above 450)

All earlier measures continue. Additionally: restrictions on truck traffic entering
Delhi other than those carrying essential commodities or running on cleaner fuels, a halt
to non-essential construction including public projects, and state discretion on office
attendance and school closure. Citizens are advised to remain indoors as far as possible.

## Why forecasting matters to GRAP

Because the measures are graded and take time to take effect, a forecast that AQI will
cross a stage threshold in 24 to 72 hours allows the stage to be invoked in advance. A
purely reactive index can only confirm that a threshold has already been crossed.
